What is an artificial neuron?
A unit that computes a weighted sum of its inputs plus a bias, then applies an activation function.
Think: sum, then squash.
What is a weight?
A learned parameter that scales one input to a neuron; larger magnitude means more influence.
What does ReLU compute?
max(0, x) — it passes positive values unchanged and zeros out negatives.
Why do networks need non-linear activations?
Without them, stacked layers collapse to a single linear map and cannot model curved decision boundaries.
What does backpropagation do?
It computes the gradient of the loss with respect to every weight so gradient descent can update them.
What is a loss function?
A scalar measure of how wrong the network's predictions are; training minimises it.
